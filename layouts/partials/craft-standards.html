Craft standards

The anatomy of a door that glides for years.

A sliding door weighs between 80 and 220 pounds. Quiet, effortless movement comes down to structural mounting and thoughtful joinery.

Structure

Kiln-dried solid timber

Hollow-core doors telegraph sound and box-store softwood slabs frequently warp under indoor winter heating. Kiln-dried hardwood such as white oak, American walnut, or clear fir stays far more stable. Which species suits your room is worth settling early; what Jeff stocks or sources is his to confirm.

Mounting

Solid header ledger beam

A heavy door should never hang from drywall anchors. A continuous solid header board anchored into several framing studs gives the rail a flat, rigid mounting plane and prevents sag. Whether your wall needs one is a site question.

Hardware

Machined bearing rollers

Sealed ball bearings in the rollers are what buy a quiet, single-finger glide; dry or open bearings rumble. Soft-close dampers at each end stop the door banging into the stops. Which hardware is on the table is a question for Jeff.

Alignment

Concealed mortise floor stay

Something has to stop the door swinging at the bottom. A slot routed into the underside of the slab, riding over a low guide pin, avoids the channels and brackets that cross a walkway. Which approach suits your floor is worth asking about.
